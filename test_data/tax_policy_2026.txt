# Tax Policy Analysis 2026

## Executive Summary

This document provides a comprehensive analysis of current tax policies and their implications for individuals and businesses in 2026.

## Individual Income Tax

### Tax Brackets and Rates
The 2026 individual income tax brackets are structured as follows:
- 10% on income up to $11,000
- 12% on income $11,001 to $44,725
- 22% on income $44,726 to $95,375
- 24% on income $95,376 to $182,100
- 32% on income $182,101 to $231,250
- 35% on income $231,251 to $578,125
- 37% on income over $578,125

Note: These rates differ slightly from previous documentation due to inflation adjustments.

### Standard Deductions
- Single filers: $13,850
- Married filing jointly: $27,700
- Head of household: $20,800

### Capital Gains
Long-term capital gains tax rates:
- 0% for income up to $44,625
- 15% for income $44,626 to $492,300
- 20% for income over $492,300

Short-term capital gains taxed at ordinary income rates.

## Corporate Taxation

### Federal Corporate Income Tax
- Flat rate: 21% on all taxable income
- No alternative minimum tax for corporations since 2017

### International Provisions
- Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income (GILTI) tax
- Foreign-Derived Intangible Income (FDII) deduction
- Base Erosion and Anti-Abuse Tax (BEAT)

## Estate and Gift Tax

### Estate Tax
- Exemption amount: $12.92 million (2026)
- Top tax rate: 40%

### Gift Tax
- Annual exclusion: $17,000 per recipient
- Lifetime exemption unified with estate tax: $12.92 million

## Retirement Accounts

### 401(k) Limits
- Employee contribution limit: $22,500
- Catch-up contribution (50+): $7,500
- Total contribution limit (including employer): $66,000

### IRA Limits
- Traditional and Roth IRA: $6,500
- Catch-up contribution (50+): $1,000

### Income Limits for Roth IRA
- Single filers phase-out: $138,000 - $153,000
- Married filing jointly phase-out: $218,000 - $228,000

## Education-Related Tax Benefits

### 529 Plans
- No federal tax deduction for contributions
- Earnings grow tax-free
- Qualified distributions tax-free

### Education Credits
- American Opportunity Tax Credit: up to $2,500 per student
- Lifetime Learning Credit: up to $2,000 per return

## Business Deductions

### Section 179 Expensing
- Maximum deduction: $1,160,000
- Phase-out threshold: $2,890,000

### Bonus Depreciation
- 100% bonus depreciation for qualified property
- Phases down starting 2023

## State and Local Tax (SALT) Deduction
- Limited to $10,000 per year for combined property and income/sales taxes

## Key Differences from Previous Year

1. **Inflation Adjustments**: All brackets, deductions, and exemptions increased by approximately 7%
2. **Retirement Contribution Limits**: 401(k) limit increased from $20,500 to $22,500
3. **Standard Deduction**: Increased significantly due to inflation
4. **Estate Tax Exemption**: Increased from $12.06M to $12.92M

## Compliance Deadlines

### Filing Deadlines
- Individual returns: April 15, 2026 (or next business day)
- Corporate returns: March 15, 2026 (or next business day)
- Extensions available: Form 4868 (individuals), Form 7004 (corporations)

### Estimated Tax Payments
- Q1: April 15, 2026
- Q2: June 15, 2026
- Q3: September 15, 2026
- Q4: January 15, 2027

## Planning Opportunities

1. **Accelerate Deductions**: Consider timing of charitable contributions
2. **Defer Income**: If expecting lower bracket in future years
3. **Roth Conversion**: Evaluate based on current vs future tax rates
4. **Qualified Business Income Deduction**: Up to 20% for pass-through entities

## Conclusion

The 2026 tax landscape presents both challenges and opportunities for taxpayers. Regular consultation with tax professionals is recommended to optimize tax positions and ensure compliance with all applicable regulations.
