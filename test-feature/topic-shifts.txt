Climate change is one of the most pressing challenges facing humanity today. Rising global temperatures have led to more frequent extreme weather events, including hurricanes, droughts, and heatwaves. Scientists project that without significant intervention, average temperatures could rise by 2-3 degrees Celsius by the end of the century. This would have catastrophic effects on ecosystems, agriculture, and coastal communities worldwide. The primary driver of climate change is the emission of greenhouse gases from human activities, particularly the burning of fossil fuels for energy and transportation.

Machine learning has emerged as a powerful tool for analyzing climate data and predicting future trends. Researchers use neural networks to model complex climate systems, identify patterns in historical temperature records, and forecast extreme weather events. Deep learning models can process satellite imagery to track deforestation, measure ice sheet melting, and monitor air quality in real time. These AI-driven insights help policymakers make data-informed decisions about climate adaptation and mitigation strategies.

The history of computing spans several distinct generations, each marked by transformative technological breakthroughs. The first generation used vacuum tubes for computation, leading to massive machines that filled entire rooms and consumed enormous amounts of electricity. The invention of the transistor ushered in the second generation, making computers smaller, more reliable, and more affordable. Integrated circuits defined the third generation, packing multiple transistors onto a single silicon chip. The microprocessor revolution of the fourth generation brought computing power to the desktop, fundamentally changing how people work and communicate.

Renewable energy technologies have advanced rapidly in recent years. Solar photovoltaic costs have dropped by over 80 percent in the past decade, making solar power competitive with fossil fuels in many markets. Wind turbine capacity has increased dramatically, with modern offshore turbines generating up to 15 megawatts each. Battery storage technology has improved significantly, addressing the intermittency challenge that has historically limited renewable energy adoption. Grid-scale battery installations are now being deployed at unprecedented scale, enabling higher penetration of variable renewable sources into the electricity grid.

Python has become the dominant programming language for data science and machine learning. Its extensive ecosystem of libraries, including NumPy for numerical computing, pandas for data manipulation, scikit-learn for classical machine learning, and PyTorch for deep learning, makes it the go-to choice for researchers and practitioners. The language's readability and simplicity lower the barrier to entry for newcomers, while its performance-critical operations are implemented in optimized C and CUDA code. Python's community support and extensive documentation have contributed to its widespread adoption across academia and industry.

International climate agreements provide the framework for global cooperation on emissions reduction. The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015 by 196 parties, aims to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. Countries submit nationally determined contributions outlining their emissions reduction targets and climate adaptation plans. However, current commitments under the Paris Agreement are insufficient to meet its temperature goals, highlighting the need for more ambitious action. The annual Conference of the Parties meetings serve as a forum for negotiating stronger commitments and reviewing progress.
